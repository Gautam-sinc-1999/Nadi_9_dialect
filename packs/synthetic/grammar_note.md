# Nadi-9 Grammar Note

- **Author:** Dr. Prakash Menon, Nadi-9 Documentation Project
- **Date:** 2023-06-15
- **Scope:** Word order, tense, negation, questions, politeness, kinship, code-switching, numbers.
- **Method:** Elicitation sessions with 4 speakers from one village. Examples are limited.

Each rule has a stable ID in square brackets (e.g. `[respect-2]`). Cite rules as `grammar:<id>`.

---

## [order-1] Basic word order

Nadi-9 is verb-final. The normal order is **Subject – Object – Verb**.

- `mi gedo vela.` — I am going home.
- `ama manu paka.` — Mother is cooking food.

## [order-2] Time words and address terms come first

Time words (`osa` today, `brem` tomorrow, `nesh` yesterday) normally stand at the start of the clause.
A vocative (the person being addressed, e.g. `ama`, `surat`, `dovan`) comes first and is followed by a comma.
If both occur, the vocative comes before the time word.

- `nesh mi tolv velen.` — Yesterday I went to the market.
- `surat, brem ...` — Sir, tomorrow ...

## [order-3] Modifiers precede what they modify

Adjectives come before nouns. Intensifiers (`gura` very) come before adjectives.

- `manu gura suma esha.` — The food is very good.

## [tense-1] Tense suffixes

Tense is marked by a suffix on the verb root.

| Tense   | Suffix | Example (`vel` go) |
|---------|--------|--------------------|
| Present | `-a`   | `vela` goes / is going |
| Past    | `-en`  | `velen` went |
| Future  | `-iru` | `veliru` will go |

There is no separate progressive; `vela` covers both "goes" and "is going".

## [tense-2] Copula

The copula `esh` (be) follows [tense-1]: `esha` is/am/are, `eshen` was/were, `eshiru` will be.
It stands at the end of the clause like any verb.

## [neg-1] Negation

The particle `na` is placed immediately **before** the verb.

- `mi so na deken.` — I did not see him.
- `so chenn na loba.` — He does not want money.

> **Not yet documented:** negation of future forms, and negative commands ("don't ..."). I did not collect enough data.

## [q-1] Yes/no questions

A yes/no question ends with the particle `ko`. Word order does not change.

- `tu manu khanen ko?` — Did you eat?

## [q-2] Question words

Question words: `keth` what, `kuv` where, `kesu` why, `kiro` who, `kedi` when.
They stay in the position of the element they ask about.

- `tu keth balen?` — What did you say?

> **Unclear:** whether `ko` is also used with question words. My one speaker omitted it.

## [respect-1] Two forms of "you"

- `tu` — familiar. Used for friends, equals, younger people, children.
- `tavo` — respectful. Used for parents, elders, employers, the village headman, and guests.

> Address to **elder siblings** varies between families. See [kin-1].

## [respect-2] Honorific verb suffix

When the subject is `tavo`, the verb takes the honorific suffix `-sa` **after** the tense suffix.

| Tense   | Form (`tam` come) |
|---------|-------------------|
| Present | `tamasa` |
| Past    | `tamensa` |
| Future  | `tamirusa` |

- `surat, tavo office tamensa.` — Sir, you came to the office.

## [respect-3] Honorific possessive

"Your" is `tura` (familiar) or `tavora` (respectful), matching [respect-1].

## [imp-1] Commands

- Familiar command: bare verb root. `dovan, mi ush.` — Elder brother, forgive me.
- Respectful command: root + `-sa`. `ushsa` — (please) forgive.

> Respectful commands are based on elicitation only. I have no recorded natural examples.

## [kin-1] Kinship terms

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| `dovan` | elder brother |
| `dovi` | younger brother |
| `maira` | elder sister |
| `mairi` | younger sister |
| `ama` | mother |
| `baro` | father |

> A form `dovanor` is also reported for "elder brother". I could not determine when it is used.

## [poss-1] Possessive pronouns

`mira` my, `tura` your (familiar), `tavora` your (respectful), `sora` his/her, `masra` our, `solra` their.
They come before the noun: `mira maira` — my elder sister.

## [plural-1] Plural

Nouns take `-li` for plural, also after numbers: `rill savili` — three days.

> **Unclear:** whether English loanwords take `-li`.

## [loc-1] Location

- A static location takes `-ma`: `office-ma` — in the office.
- A destination of motion is unmarked: `mi gedo vela` — I go home.

## [cs-1] Code-switching

Speakers insert English nouns for modern things (office, phone, meeting, bus, report) unchanged.
These nouns take Nadi-9 suffixes such as `-ma`.
English verbs are not used directly. Instead: English noun + `kor` (do): `meeting kora` — (I) hold a meeting.

## [num-1] Numbers

`yem` 1, `tosh` 2, `rill` 3, `kanu` 4, `pavo` 5, `dem` 10.

> Numbers 6–9 and above 10 are not documented.
